Season Outrights Model

Team ratings from goals, xG and betting lines, turned into match prices and season outcome odds

Author

Luke Chambers

1. The model

Pick a league, then use the sliders to change how many recent matches power each rating and adjust how much weight to give goals, xG and betting odds in each teams rating.

You will see the Team ratings adjust based on these inputs. This will change the match odds for every upcoming fixture for the rest of the season in Fixtures & prices, which in turn feeds into the Predicted table. Using these predicted match odds for you can run a Monte Carlo simulation in Season Odds that will show each teams chances of finishing 1st, Top 4, Top 6 and Bottom 3.

The weightings are pre-set to the optimal combination of weights and number of matches that have historically made the best predictions for each league.

The odds weight fills whatever is left so the three always add up to 1. Reset to optimised restores the optimised ratings. Your league and settings are saved in the page link, so you can share a particular set-up with someone.

2. Overview

Rating a team on goals alone is noisy, on xG alone ignores finishing and goalkeeping, and on betting lines alone simply copies the market. This model blends all three into attack and defence ratings, prices every remaining fixture from them, and simulates the rest of the season to put odds on the title, the top four, the top six and relegation. It covers the top five European leagues, updates daily, and every number in the app is recalculated in your browser as you move the sliders.

3. Data

Two sources are joined match by match:

  • Understat for each match’s goals and expected goals (xG), and this season’s remaining fixtures. It is scraped fresh for every update, back to the 2015/16 season.
  • totalcorner.com for the pre-match Asian handicap and goal line, for both the top flight and the division below. These come from the daily scrape that also feeds the corner model. The goal line is solved for the Poisson goals rate that prices it fairly, and the handicap for the goal difference that prices it fairly under a Skellam model. Together they give the market’s expected goals for each side, called Asian goals here. This is the same odds-to-goals step used in the corner model.

Understat scrape and team-name matching → code/lt/understat.py

Odds to expected goals for each side → code/lt/asian_lines.py

The two sites spell some teams differently (Manchester City and Man City), so Understat names are converted to totalcorner’s. A match is then joined to its lines by the two teams and a kick-off date within two days, since the sites can put a late kick-off either side of midnight. 99.7% of matches find their lines. A match without lines (usually one played in the last day or so, before the next scrape) still counts towards its teams’ goals and xG averages. The odds average skips it.

Matches played behind closed doors (March 2020 to June 2022) are left out. Home advantage is close to zero in them, and they would distort both the ratings and the home advantage factor.

4. Ratings

Each team gets an attack rating (goals it’s expected to score) and a defence rating (goals it’s expected to concede) against an average opponent. Each is a weighted average of three rolling means over the team’s last n league matches:

\text{Attack} = w_g\,\overline{\text{goals}} + w_x\,\overline{\text{xG}} + w_o\,\overline{\text{Asian}}

where w_g + w_x + w_o = 1, and the same for goals, xG and Asian goals conceded.

The window and weights were chosen by grid search, separately for each league. For every window from 5 to 29 matches and every weight combination, each past match was predicted using only ratings from before it. The predictions were then scored on mean absolute error (MAE) against the market’s own total and supremacy for that match. Supremacy counted for 75% of the score, since this model is mainly for finding supremacy differences. The search used seasons up to 2022/23. The winning combination was then scored on 2023/24 onwards, which the search never saw.

Grid search: find_optimal_parameters() → code/lt/optimise.py

The leagues agree closely. Every one picked a window of 16 to 18 matches with 70% to 80% of the weight on the odds. The heavy odds weight is expected, because the market already contains most of what goals and xG say about a team. Promoted teams

A newly promoted team has few top-flight matches to rate it on, and none at all at the start of the season. Its matches from the season it won promotion are used instead, with the market’s expected goals scaled to top-flight level. Each league’s scaling comes from its own past promotions (165 across the five leagues). For each one, the team’s average Asian goals in its first top-flight season are compared with its promotion season. Depending on the league, promoted teams’ expected goals scored fell by 21% to 33% and their expected goals conceded rose by 49% to 67%.

Only the odds-based metric can be carried over this way, because Understat has no second-division goals or xG. Within the window, goals and xG are averaged over the team’s top-flight matches. A team with no top-flight matches yet is rated on its scaled Asian goals alone. In the app, these teams are tagged P with the number of second-division matches in their window.

5. From ratings to prices

A fixture’s expected goals multiply one side’s attack by the other side’s defence, relative to the league’s average defence:

\lambda_{home} = \frac{\text{Att}_{home}\times\text{Def}_{away}}{\overline{\text{Def}}}

\lambda_{away} = \frac{\text{Att}_{away}\times\text{Def}_{home}}{\overline{\text{Def}}}

Home advantage is then moved from the away side to the home side. The home team gains a fixed share of the match’s expected goals (9.2% to 14.6% depending on the league) and the total stays the same. That share is the home-minus-away goal difference as a proportion of all goals in the league’s non-COVID history.

Expected goals: predict_goals() → code/lt/ratings.py

Two independent Poisson distributions turn the expected goals into a probability for every scoreline up to 10-10. Independent Poissons slightly under-price low-scoring draws, so the Dixon-Coles correction reweights 0-0, 0-1, 1-0 and 1-1 using one parameter, ρ. ρ is fitted for each league by maximum likelihood on past scorelines, with the market’s expected goals as the inputs. Adding up the scorelines where the home side wins, where it’s level, and where the away side wins gives the home, draw and away probabilities. Their inverses are the fair (margin-free) 1X2 odds.

Dixon-Coles scorelines and 1X2 → code/lt/dixon_coles.py

The predicted table adds each fixture’s expected points (3 × win probability + draw probability) and expected goals to the current table.

6. Simulating the season

Expected points give the most likely finishing order, but not how likely each outcome is. For that, the rest of the season is simulated thousands of times. Each fixture is drawn as a home win, draw or away win from its 1X2 probabilities, and the final positions are recorded. The share of simulations in which a team wins the league, finishes in the top 4 or top 6, or ends in the bottom 3 is its probability, and 1 / probability is its fair price.
